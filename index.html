Walks · 5–15 km · under two hours by bike

Lock the bike,
and walk.

Nineteen documented walking routes of 5 to 15 km, every one of them within cycling reach of Amstelveen — the furthest trailhead sits at 29.6 km, comfortably inside two hours at an easy pace.

Cycling distances are measured from central Amstelveen using a bicycle profile on OpenStreetMap data (BRouter); ride times assume 16 km/h. The Fietsersbond route planner gives better cycling routes — click a trailhead’s coordinates to copy them and plan the ride there.

Before you set off

  • Botshol and Waverveen are out this year. The fine routes at Botshol (12 km) and Trage Tocht Waverveen (15 km) are closed for embankment repairs — sources put the reopening no earlier than late 2026, possibly mid-2027. Vinkeveen and Abcoude below are the stand-ins.
  • Breeding season closes paths. Peat-meadow and marsh reserves shut roughly 15 March – 15 June (Het Twiske until 15 July). At Loenen, the Vrederijk estate closes 1 April – 31 July. Noted on each route.
  • The Waterleidingduinen charge admission. €1.50 day ticket or €10 annual card from age 18, children free; no dogs and no bikes inside, but there are free bike racks at every entrance. During the rut (September–November) keep well clear of the fallow deer.
  • Anything north of the IJ needs the ferry. Zunderdorp, Broek in Waterland and Het Twiske all go via the free ferry behind Amsterdam Centraal (Buiksloterweg or IJplein, 24/7, bikes free). That crossing is included in the cycling distance.
  • GPX access is uneven, and that is on the publishers. Natuurmonumenten, Wandelnet, the AWD, Laag Holland and the Plaece-based tourist sites give the track away free. Wandelzoekpagina — which owns the Trage Tocht and Kort en Prachtig series, six routes here — puts GPX and the route description behind €13.49 a year or €2.49 for a single route. Where that bites, each card links a free lookalike track on gps-route.org, but read the distance: it is a different route design, not the same walk.
  • Ten of the nineteen carry a real track. Where a card shows a shape, it was parsed from that route's own GPX — projected, simplified and then re-measured as a check. All ten came back within a few hundred metres of their published length, which is the useful part: a shape that measures right is a shape you can trust. Click any shape for the full map: the track is drawn over OpenStreetMap tiles, and you can pan and zoom. If the tiles cannot load — say, offline — the map still opens with the bare track drawn large and clean. The nine without a shape are the ones whose GPX is paywalled or does not exist.
  • Trage Tocht routes are not waymarked. Download the GPX or use the WandelZapp app; and in the wet months a laarzenpad (boots path) is exactly that.
